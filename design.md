# QBF Wireless Split Perkins Keyboard

1.  This is a Perkins style keyboard.
    1.  8 keys are mapped to the 8 braille dots that make up a Braille cell.
    2.  These keys can be pressed together in different combinations to type
        different Braille cells.
    3.  They are pressed at the same time as part of a chord.
    4.  The space bar may also be used as part of chords, commonly for computer
        interaction.

## Outline

1.  Boards that have influenced this one:
    1.  Sofle Choc is comfortable. I've adopted it's spacing, minimal stagger,
        and low profile switches. But it's wired, so not conveniently portable.
    2.  ChocV has a great base plate, low profile, and very portable. But no
        display makes wireless configuration a guessing game.
    3.  Toucan has great grip pads. But a stagger that I, unfortunately, find
        unusable. The cirque trackpad was ok, but not great. The v2 rectangular
        one I've adopted.

## Component choices

1.  1.5U keycaps
    1.  The board doesn't have a columnar stagger because it wants to maintain
        symmetry.
    2.  But people do have different length fingers and preferences for stagger.
    3.  A 1.5U keycap gives a bit more allowance for that variation, without
        needing stabilisers.
2.  Choc v1
    1.  The lowest commonly available switch.
    2.  Low profile boards are comfortable.
    3.  Small boards are portable.
3.  nice!nano v2 controller
    1.  Through-hole sockets expose enough pins for ten direct keys and a display.
4.  Ready-made nice!view display
    1.  Low power, through-hole connections, and documented use in HillSideView.
